- Raw CSV/JSON: `results/<profile>/metrics/`

This document extracts the experiment-level results and states what each one shows.

**Common setup (MITSUI).** 424 targets (106 per horizon, h = 1–4, gap 1).

- Development walk-forward: date_id 630–1460 (831 out-of-sample dates).
- **Final holdout: date_id 1461–1960 (500 dates)**, evaluated once after the design freeze.
- Expanding window, horizon-specific label-availability purge.
- Score = mean/std of the daily Spearman IC across all targets.
- CIs: circular block bootstrap (block 20). t: Newey–West HAC.

Development numbers are optimistic for tuned models and in-sample for ensemble weights.

**Coverage.** FAST and FULL both evaluate all 424 targets; FAST is cheaper only through
refit cadence, grid sizes and LSTM seeds. Nothing was marked incomplete: every planned
experiment ran in both profiles.

## Experiment F: pooled/factorised vs target-specific

| Model | Dev score | Holdout score | Holdout mean IC | t |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ridge (asset-factorised, pooled) | 0.045 | −0.083 | −0.022 | −1.18 |
| ridge_direct_pooled (pair features, pooled per horizon) | −0.007 | 0.006 | 0.001 | 0.08 |
| ridge_direct_target (one ridge per target) | −0.034 | **0.203** | **0.030** | **3.05** |

Holdout paired: ridge − ridge_direct_target = −0.053 [−0.088, −0.012], t = −2.50.

**Finding (exploratory).** On development the factorised model ranked first and the
per-target model last. On the holdout the order reversed. The per-target model's holdout
skill is reproduced by its intercept alone, a target-level expanding mean (holdout IC
+0.039, t = 2.79; development −0.010). That reflects pair-level persistence in 2023–25,
e.g. JPX gold legs moved +8.1 bps/day in gold's favour on the holdout vs +1.7 on
development. With about 17 models compared, this single significant result is not evidence of
a robust edge, and it would not have been selected by the protocol.

**What FULL changes.**

1. Every FAST conclusion about RQ1 and RQ2 holds. No pre-specified learned model or
   ensemble is significant, and cross-asset features help on development only.
2. The regime pattern (RQ3) is sharper for Ridge, the LSTM and the ensemble: positive IC in
   high-volatility, negative in low-volatility regimes.
3. With more frequent refits, regime-conditional weighting beat equal weighting by +0.007
   (p = 0.09). That is the strongest evidence in the project for RQ4a, but it is not
   significant at 5% and the FAST profile showed −0.002. It remains a hypothesis to test on
   new data.
4. The 3-seed LSTM produced the best gross paper Sharpe (1.46, about 2 standard errors).
   That is one of 9 signals tested, its rank IC is not significant, and it falls to 0.07 at
   5 bps. It is noted, not claimed.
5. In every FULL refit the LSTM's early stopping chose the first epoch: with these features,
   the purged validation loss never improved after one pass over the data.
